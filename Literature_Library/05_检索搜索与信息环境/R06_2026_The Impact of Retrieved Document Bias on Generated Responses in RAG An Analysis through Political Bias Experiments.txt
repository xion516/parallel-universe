          Abstr act     This study investigates the transfer of political bias from retrieved documents to
        responses generated by Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)-based Large Language Models
        (LLMs). While prior research has primarily focused on biases within retrievers or LLMs themselves,
        the impact of document bias has remained largely unexplored. To address this gap, we empirically
        assessed    how   political   biases   from   documents-categorized   as   Left,   Center,   or   Right-influence
        generated responses. Our results confirm significant bias transfer: politically biased documents
        noticeably influenced the responses. Specifically, when a right-leaning LLM referenced left-biased
        documents, its responses shifted toward left bias. Conversely, referencing additional right-leaning
        documents had only a minimal effect on responses, suggesting the presence of pre-existing internal
        biases within the LLM. These findings underscore that document biases significantly shape RAG
        outputs. This highlights the necessity of comprehensive strategies that account for the complex
        interactions among retrievers, model biases, and document biases to effectively mitigate overall system
        bias. Such an approach is crucial for developing balanced and reliable RAG-based systems.
          Keywords:       RAG, LLM, AI ethics, retrieved document bias
